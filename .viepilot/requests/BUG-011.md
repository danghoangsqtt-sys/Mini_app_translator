# 🐛 BUG: Stale delayed `Handler` runnable can fire after `ConversationService` teardown

## Meta
- **ID**: BUG-011
- **Type**: Bug
- **Status**: new
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Created**: 2026-09-19
- **Affected**: `voice_translation/_conversation_mode/_conversation/ConversationService.java:57` (`static Handler mHandler`), `:348-354` (post), `:308-325` (`onDestroy`)

## Summary
`onScoAudioDisconnected()` posts a 1-second-delayed `stop()/start()` on `BluetoothHelper` via a `static Handler`. `onDestroy()` never calls `mHandler.removeCallbacksAndMessages(null)`, so a service destroyed within that 1-second window still runs the callback against a torn-down helper/service.

## Actual Behavior
Delayed callback can execute after the service and its `BluetoothHelper` have already been torn down, risking a crash or use of stale references. The `static` nature of `mHandler` is also an Android-lint-flagged leak pattern.

## Expected Behavior
`onDestroy()` should call `mHandler.removeCallbacksAndMessages(null)` before the service finishes tearing down; consider making the handler non-static (tied to the service instance) instead.

## Steps to Reproduce
1. Trigger a SCO audio disconnect event during an active conversation (posts the delayed runnable).
2. Destroy the service (e.g. user ends conversation) within 1 second of that event.
3. Observe the delayed callback executing against a torn-down `BluetoothHelper`.

## Error Messages
Likely `NullPointerException` or similar, timing-dependent.
