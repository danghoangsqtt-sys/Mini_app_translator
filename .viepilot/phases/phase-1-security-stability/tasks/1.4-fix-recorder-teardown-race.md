# Task 1.4 — Fix recorder teardown race

## Objective

Ensure an `AudioRecord` is released only by its capture thread after a stop request has unblocked the active read and that thread has exited. Prevent a replacement recording session from starting while the former reader is still alive.

## File-Level Plan

| File | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Recorder.java` | Serialize start/stop, request shutdown, call `AudioRecord.stop()` to unblock `read()`, join the reader, and let the reader release its own local `AudioRecord` in `finally`. Stop processing on non-positive reads. | Removes concurrent read/release and prevents session fields being swapped beneath a reader. |
| `RecordingSessionController.java` | Add a small pure-Java stop-then-interrupt-then-join coordinator with a bounded wait and interrupt preservation. | Makes the critical ordering independently testable and prevents indefinite UI blocking. |
| `RecordingSessionControllerTest.java` | Stress repeated blocked-read shutdowns and assert stop happens before reader-owned release and no reader survives completion. | Reproduces the lifecycle race without depending on microphone hardware. |

## Implementation Notes

- `AudioRecord.stop()` may be invoked from the owner of the recorder to unblock a blocking `read()`; `release()` remains exclusively owned by the capture thread.
- A timed-out reader is retained and no new recording session is created over it. Its `finally` block will release the resource if it later exits.
- The capture loop checks the stop flag immediately after `read()` and ignores error/empty reads.
- Preserve existing callback behavior for normal start/stop flows.
